# ComputerVision_Project
## The following project aims to solve a research based problem from the roots of backbone of CNNs and NMS suppression on the Indian Driving Dataset where the classes are misclassified and False positives and Negatives comes to be significantly high which we try to move and mitigate the issues regarding our research based track by the following methods.

### We present a five-stage forensic ablation study of objectdetection failure in the Indian Driving Dataset (IDD), an environment characterised by extreme spatial occlusion (IoU>0.8) and absent lane discipline, a domain shift that violates the spatial-separation assumptions of COCO-trained models. On a curated adversarial subset of ∼4000 images, we employ a deliberate process of elimination: each stage (DIoU-NMS → Faster R-CNN → FocalNet → RT-DETR → CBAM) is designed not to fix the model, but to prove that a specific candidate cause cannot explain the failure. The result is a negative proof: a terminal False-Negative Rate (FNR) of 9.4% at extreme overlap is inelastic to every software-level intervention tested, including a ∼850% increase in compute. Grad-CAM and TIDE forensics localise the failure to the CNN backbone, a representational phenomenon we term Feature Entanglement. We conclude that the bounding-box paradigm reaches a geometric limit at >80% occlusion, where spatial coordinates for distinct object identities achieve mathematical parity, and that recovery requires pixel-level instance segmentation.
